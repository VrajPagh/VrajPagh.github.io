Design Evolution

1. Initial Puncher Concept

Add the first robot photo here.

The first build used a simple puncher mechanism to prove the scoring concept and establish a baseline.

2. Elevated Puncher Prototype

Add the elevated-puncher robot photo here.

We raised the puncher to improve release angle and clearance. Testing showed that the higher center of gravity and increased complexity outweighed the benefits.

3. Lower Puncher Redesign

Add the lower-puncher redesign photo here.

We returned to a lower puncher to improve stability, simplify packaging, and make maintenance easier.

4. Competition Refinement

Add the fourth robot iteration photo here.

Later iterations focused on weight distribution, structural rigidity, drivetrain response, and repeatable scoring.

5. Final Competition Robot

Add the final robot photo here.

The final design used a compact lower puncher and a C-tier hanging mechanism, balancing scoring consistency, stability, serviceability, and driver control.

Programming and Testing

As the programmer, I developed autonomous routines and driver-control behavior, debugged mechanical and software interactions, and adjusted the program through repeated field testing.

Results

The completed robot earned a 200-point Skills Challenge score, placed in the Top 10 in New Jersey, and qualified for the New Jersey VEX State Championship.

What I Learned

This season taught me that a more complicated design is not automatically better. Removing the elevated puncher showed the importance of comparing performance, stability, maintenance, and reliability before committing to a mechanism.
